# Dimuon slide briefing

Use this briefing for every slide outline and deck in the dimuon heavy-ion project.

## Visual and editorial style

- Make academic physics research slides for a scientific audience.
- Be minimal, direct, and evidence-led: white background, restrained typography, sparse color, and generous whitespace.
- Avoid large color bars, decorative banners, gradients, stock imagery, flashy transitions, and ornamental visuals.
- Start a new deck with a title slide.
- Default content slide: one dominant plot or tightly related plot group, plus a short factual caption or takeaway. Keep text subordinate to evidence.
- Emphasize methodology, validation, uncertainties, scientific results, and current status. Use precise qualifiers such as preliminary, uncorrected, closure, statistical-only, or work in progress when supported by the provided context.
- Do not invent an ATLAS status label, physics interpretation, uncertainty statement, plot caption, or conclusion.

## Group and analysis meetings

Audience: the user's mentors and collaborators on this analysis. They already know the analysis, codebase, samples, selections, and prior decisions very well.

- Present only the most recent progress, changed methodology, validation, blockers, decisions needed, and current status.
- Do not explain basic heavy-ion physics, ATLAS, the analysis motivation, standard definitions, or implementation details unless explicitly requested.
- Organize the scientific body as a compact methodology/status overview followed by results.
- Use the most recent applicable analysis-meeting deck from Google Drive/Slides as the layout template.
- Favor a title slide followed by plot-led slides with brief captions; omit agenda and background slides unless they materially help the update.

## EW/Onia meetings

Audience: ATLAS Heavy-Ion Electroweak/Quarkonia subgroup conveners and members. They know heavy-ion physics, ATLAS, electroweak physics, muons, and heavy flavor very well, but do not know this analysis's specific workflow and current internal state.

- Keep field-wide and detector background terse.
- Explain analysis-specific procedure, physics motivation, or context only when the user's prompt explicitly asks for it.
- When such context is requested, make it a compact bridge to the plots rather than a tutorial.
- Center the deck on the requested methodology, evidence, results, validation, and decision or request.

## ATLAS approval meetings

Audience: ATLAS heavy-ion experts in editorial-board, group-approval, or similar approval settings. They know heavy-ion physics, ATLAS, muons, and heavy flavor well, but not this analysis's specifics.

- Do not teach standard detector or heavy-ion concepts.
- Give enough analysis-specific motivation, procedure, validation, and uncertainty context for technical scrutiny.
- Make analysis logic and claims auditable: show selections, closure/validation, systematic reasoning, and result status when requested or needed to understand the presented result.
- Keep the visual treatment minimal and plot-led; rigor should come from content, not denser decoration.
